Choosing an auspicious day

Traditional wedding-date selection often begins by removing clearly unfavorable days, then favoring days that support the couple’s signs. The filters below are the main exclusion and preference rules used with this calendar.

Can Chi

The Can Chi system combines two systems: the ten Heavenly Stems and the twelve Earthly Branches. Each system independently assigns both a Yin–Yang polarity and an element from the Five Elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water). In the Heavenly Stems, the Five Elements are arranged as five pairs, with each element appearing once in its Yang form and once in its Yin form, producing the ten Stems. The Earthly Branches follow a twelve-position cycle in which Yin and Yang alternate, while each Branch is also assigned one of the Five Elements according to its traditional elemental association; each Branch is additionally represented by a zodiac animal. When a Stem and Branch are combined, their polarities always agree—Yang with Yang and Yin with Yin—but they can contribute different elements. A Can Chi designation therefore contains a Stem element and a Branch element, together with their shared Yin or Yang polarity and the zodiac animal associated with the Branch.

Năm Tuổi

Năm Tuổi (Birth-Zodiac Year) occurs when the zodiac animal of the current lunar year is the same as a person's birth-year zodiac. Because the twelve Earthly Branches repeat every twelve years, a Năm Tuổi returns at twelve-year intervals and is traditionally regarded as a period of heightened change or uncertainty. For important life events such as marriage, some Vietnamese families therefore prefer to avoid a year that is the Năm Tuổi of either the bride or groom.

Ghost Month

The Ghost Month (Tháng Cô Hồn) traditionally refers to the seventh month of the Vietnamese lunar calendar, a period associated with wandering spirits and remembrance of the dead. The month reaches its spiritual height around the 15th lunar day, during Rằm Tháng Bảy, when families make offerings to ancestors and wandering spirits and observe the Buddhist festival of Vu Lan. Because the month is traditionally regarded as a spiritually unsettled time, many Vietnamese families avoid beginning major undertakings such as weddings, moving into a new home, opening a business, or making significant purchases. For traditional wedding-date selection, the entire seventh lunar month may therefore be excluded or treated as strongly unfavorable, although customs and the degree of observance vary among families and regions.

Tam Nương

Tam Nương (三娘), meaning “Three Ladies,” refers to a set of traditionally inauspicious days that occur every month in the Vietnamese lunar calendar. The 3rd, 7th, 13th, 18th, 22nd, and 27th lunar days are designated as Tam Nương days and are commonly avoided when choosing dates for important beginnings, particularly weddings, construction, moving, business openings, and major journeys. The tradition is popularly associated with three legendary women— Muội Hỷ, Đát Kỷ, and Bao Tự—whose stories became symbols of disruption and misfortune. In traditional date selection, a Tam Nương designation therefore acts as a warning against beginning an important undertaking on that day, even when other characteristics of the date may appear favorable.

Nguyệt Kỵ

Nguyệt Kỵ (月忌), meaning “Monthly Taboo,” refers to three traditionally inauspicious dates that occur in every month of the Vietnamese lunar calendar: the 5th, 14th, and 23rd lunar days. These dates have long been regarded as unfavorable for important beginnings and major undertakings, particularly weddings, long journeys, business transactions, construction, and other significant events. Unlike classifications based on the Heavenly Stems or Earthly Branches, Nguyệt Kỵ is determined solely by the numbered day of the lunar month, making it one of the simplest traditional almanac rules to identify. In date selection, these three dates are therefore commonly avoided even when other characteristics of the day appear favorable.

Taboo Almanac Days

Major Taboo Almanac Days are dates traditionally regarded as particularly unfavorable within the Vietnamese lunar calendar and are generally avoided when planning important events. These designations come from traditional almanac systems that evaluate the lunar month, day, and calendrical cycles to identify periods associated with obstruction, loss, instability, or an unfavorable beginning. Because weddings represent the beginning of a new household and family life, dates carrying a major taboo are typically removed from consideration before more favorable qualities of the remaining dates are evaluated. In this way, taboo days function as an initial exclusionary filter rather than simply a negative factor to be balanced against otherwise auspicious characteristics.

Conflicting Signs

Remove days whose lunar-day Earthly Branch directly opposes the year Earthly Branch of either person.

Hắc Đạo

Hắc Đạo (黑道), literally the “Black Path,” refers to days considered generally inauspicious within the traditional Vietnamese almanac and forms the counterpart to Hoàng Đạo. A Hắc Đạo day is governed by one of six unfavorable celestial spirits: Thiên Hình (天刑), Heavenly Punishment, associated with punishment and conflict; Chu Tước (朱雀), the Vermilion Bird, associated with disputes and discord; Bạch Hổ (白虎), the White Tiger, associated with danger and injury; Thiên Lao (天牢), Heavenly Prison, associated with restriction and obstruction; Huyền Vũ (玄武), the Dark Warrior, associated with secrecy, deception, and uncertainty; and Câu Trần (勾陳), Gouchen, associated with entanglement, delay, and difficulty. Like Hoàng Đạo, the governing spirit is determined by the relationship between the lunar month and the Earthly Branch of the day. Hắc Đạo days are generally avoided for important beginnings such as weddings, although they are best understood as an unfavorable almanac classification rather than an absolute prohibition.

Hoàng Đạo

Hoàng Đạo (黃道), literally the “Yellow Path,” refers to days considered generally auspicious within the traditional Vietnamese almanac. Each day is associated with one of a cycle of celestial spirits, six of which are regarded as favorable: Thanh Long (青龍), the Azure Dragon, associated with success and favorable beginnings; Minh Đường (明堂), the Bright Hall, associated with clarity and prosperity; Kim Quỹ (金匱), the Golden Coffer, associated with wealth, resources, and preservation; Thiên Đức (天德), Heavenly Virtue, associated with protection, benevolence, and good fortune; Ngọc Đường (玉堂), the Jade Hall, associated with harmony, celebration, and favorable relationships; and Tư Mệnh (司命), the Controller of Destiny, associated with life, well-being, and the orderly course of affairs. A day governed by one of these six auspicious spirits is classified as a Hoàng Đạo day and is generally preferred for important occasions. The governing spirit is determined from the relationship between the lunar month and the Earthly Branch of the day, rather than from the person's zodiac or Nạp Âm. For wedding-date selection, Hoàng Đạo provides a strong positive indication, although the date should still be checked against major taboo days and conflicts with the couple.

Choosing the best date

After eliminating dates considered generally unfavorable, choosing the best date depends on which factors you are trying to balance or emphasize. A date is not simply “good” or “bad” in isolation; its suitability depends on how its Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Yin–Yang polarity, and Five Elements interact with the person and the purpose of the event. Different dates may strengthen, weaken, support, clash with, or balance different parts of a person’s Four Pillars (BaZi). Therefore, the preferred date depends on what matters most for the particular activity—for example, relationships and marriage, career and business, family and children, health and stability, or long-term development. This evaluation can be understood through the four pillars of a person’s birth chart:

  • Year Pillar — Ancestry & Outer World: Represents family heritage, ancestral influences, early environment, and the person’s relationship with the broader social world.
  • Month Pillar — Environment & Elemental Strength: Represents the seasonal environment at birth and plays a major role in determining the relative strength, weakness, and influence of the Five Elements within the chart.
  • Day Pillar — Self & Relationships: Forms the personal center of the Four Pillars. Its Heavenly Stem represents the self, known as the Day Master, while its Earthly Branch is traditionally associated with the spouse, partnership, and close relationships.
  • Hour Pillar — Aspirations & Later Life: Represents ambitions, inner aspirations, future development, children and legacy, and influences traditionally understood to become more prominent later in life.
