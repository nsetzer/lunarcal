Heavenly Stems

The ten Heavenly Stems (Thiên can) cycle with the twelve animals to name years and other pillars. Each element appears twice — Yang then Yin — so the full stem–branch calendar repeats every 60 years.

Zodiac animals

The twelve animals follow the Earthly Branch order (Địa chi), a fixed cycle used for years, months, days, and hours: Rat, Buffalo, Tiger, Cat, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig. Folklore ties that sequence to the Jade Emperor’s race — the Rat finished first by riding the Buffalo’s back, and the rest arrived in the places shown below. In the Vietnamese zodiac the fourth place is the Cat (Mão), where Chinese tradition uses the Rabbit.

Friends are triad allies (Tam hợp): four groups of three animals spaced four years apart that support one another

  • Rat–Dragon–Monkey
  • Buffalo–Snake–Rooster
  • Tiger–Horse–Dog
  • Cat–Goat–Pig

Enemies follow Tứ Hành Xung (“four conflicting signs”): three groups of four animals whose energies are traditionally considered mutually incompatible. Members of a group sit three steps apart on the zodiac circle (not merely the animal directly opposite, six steps away). This is a cultural compatibility belief often cited for relationships and partnerships, not a scientific system.

  • Rat–Horse–Cat–Rooster
  • Buffalo–Goat–Dragon–Dog
  • Tiger–Monkey–Snake–Pig
