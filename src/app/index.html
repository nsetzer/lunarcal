VN Lunar Calendar

In Vietnamese tradition, the Tết Lunar Calendar is used instead of the Chinese lunar calendar. Although the two calendars share a common historical foundation, Vietnam adapted the system over centuries to reflect its own cultural traditions, symbolism, and ways of understanding the natural world. Most notably, the Vietnamese zodiac uses the Buffalo and Cat in place of the Chinese Ox and Rabbit, and follows a different rotation in the assignment of the Five Elements.

Within the Vietnamese lunar calendar, years are organized according to the Sexagenary Cycle, a repeating cycle of sixty combinations. Each position in the cycle is formed by pairing one of the Ten Heavenly Stems with one of the Twelve Earthly Branches. Together, these provide each year with its elemental and zodiac characteristics.

The same system can be applied more precisely to the year, month, day, and hour of birth. These four points form the Four Pillars, with each pillar consisting of a Heavenly Stem and an Earthly Branch. Each Stem and Branch also carries a Yin or Yang polarity, adding another dimension to the elemental and zodiac associations of each pillar. Taken together, the Four Pillars provide a traditional framework for interpreting the elemental and zodiac influences associated with the moment of a person's birth.

Appendix

The Five Elements—Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water—describe five fundamental forms of energy within the Vietnamese traditional system. Rather than representing physical substances alone, each element expresses a set of qualities, tendencies, and relationships found in nature and human life. The elements interact through repeating cycles of generation and control: each element can nourish and strengthen another, while also regulating or restraining another. Within the Four Pillars, the balance and interaction of these five elements provide a framework for understanding how different energies support, oppose, or influence one another.

Heavenly Stems

The Ten Heavenly Stems (Thiên Can) represent the Five Elements expressed through their Yin and Yang polarities. Each of the five elements—Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water—appears twice: once in a Yang form and once in a Yin form, creating the ten Stems.

In Vietnamese tradition, a person is also associated with an Elemental Destiny (Nạp Âm), which gives a more specific expression to the Five Elements. Rather than identifying a person simply as Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, or Water, Nạp Âm assigns a distinctive elemental form to each pair of years within the sixty-year Sexagenary Cycle. These forms are expressed through imagery drawn from the natural world, such as Fire in the Furnace, Water of the Great Sea, or Earth by the Roadside. For example, 1990—the year of Canh Ngọ (Yang Metal Horse)—has the Elemental Destiny Lộ Bàng Thổ, or Earth by the Roadside. Although the year's Heavenly Stem is Metal, its Nạp Âm destiny is Earth, illustrating that Elemental Destiny is a separate layer of interpretation derived from the complete Stem–Branch combination.

Earthly Branches

The Twelve Earthly Branches (Địa Chi) are represented by the twelve animals of the Vietnamese zodiac: Rat, Buffalo, Tiger, Cat, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. Like the Heavenly Stems, the Earthly Branches follow an alternating Yin and Yang polarity, beginning with the Yang Rat and continuing in sequence through the twelve animals. When the Twelve Earthly Branches are paired with the Ten Heavenly Stems, their matching Yin or Yang polarities produce the sixty unique combinations of the Sexagenary Cycle.

Friends are triad allies (Tam hợp): four groups of three animals spaced four years apart that support one another

  • Rat–Dragon–Monkey
  • Buffalo–Snake–Rooster
  • Tiger–Horse–Dog
  • Cat–Goat–Pig

Enemies follow Tứ Hành Xung (“four conflicting signs”): three groups of four animals whose energies are traditionally considered mutually incompatible. Members of a group sit three steps apart on the zodiac circle (not merely the animal directly opposite, six steps away). This is a cultural compatibility belief often cited for relationships and partnerships, not a scientific system.

  • Rat–Horse–Cat–Rooster
  • Buffalo–Goat–Dragon–Dog
  • Tiger–Monkey–Snake–Pig
